Where to mine

TargetProsCons
Moon (regolith)Close (days away). Roughly 40% oxygen by mass, plus silicon, aluminium, iron, magnesium, calcium. Ilmenite (FeTiO₃) in the maria gives titanium and iron. Many planned landers.Deep gravity well to land on. Abrasive dust. Low titanium concentration compared with ore on Earth, so processing is energy-hungry. 14-day nights.
Near-Earth asteroidsNo deep gravity well. Some are metal-rich. Easy to move material once extracted.Hard to reach, tiny gravity, unproven extraction. Long mission times. Silicate asteroids are less well characterised.

Recommendation: start on the Moon. Demand will come from lunar bases and in-orbit construction, and the regolith is a known quantity.

What the product is

  • Oxygen (most abundant, needed for life support and propellant).
  • Construction blocks from sintered regolith (lowest tech, earliest sale).
  • Metals and silicon (aluminium, iron, titanium, silicon for solar cells). Highest value, hardest process.

Titanium and silicon are the most valuable but need electrolysis or carbothermal processes. The sensible order is blocks first, oxygen second, metals third.

Most competitors sell Earth-bound materials. Few focus on selling building materials to customers in space, which is the gap this idea targets.

Law

The Outer Space Treaty bans national appropriation of celestial bodies, but the US Commercial Space Launch Competitiveness Act (2015), Luxembourg's 2017 law and the Artemis Accords support the right to own resources you extract. This is a developing area; get a space lawyer before anything real.

Risks

  • No customers yet. Demand depends on lunar bases being funded.
  • Capital intensity, years before revenue.
  • Technology readiness: in-situ processing is mostly demonstrated only in labs.
  • Launch and landing reliability are outside your control.

Cheapest first milestone

Build a small regolith-simulant processing rig on Earth that produces oxygen or sintered bricks, with data on energy per kilogram. That is a few thousand to tens of thousands of dollars, a proof for grants (NASA SBIR/STTR, ESA) and the basis for a first customer conversation.
