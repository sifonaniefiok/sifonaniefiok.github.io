What AI Actually Means for People Like Us

March 2026 · by Sifon Imahjnr

The conversation about AI is happening without us.

Not because we aren't affected, we are, more than most.

But because the people setting the terms of the debate

are not us. They are not young. They are not from here.

They are not building their future with one hand

while studying with the other, in a country where the

infrastructure was never designed for what they are

trying to do.

So let me say what they are not saying.

This Is Not Science Fiction

The first thing to understand about AI is that it is

already here, already working, already making decisions

that affect your life, and it has been for years.

The algorithm that decides which content you see.

The system that scores your loan application.

The tool that filters your CV before a human ever

reads it. These are not future threats. They are

present realities. And if you have been waiting for

AI to become relevant to your life, I want to tell

you gently: you missed the memo.

What is new is not AI. What is new is that AI has

become visible. The tools are now in our hands,

not just in the hands of corporations and governments.

And that changes something important.

What It Actually Takes Away

Let me be direct about the losses first, because

the optimism can wait.

AI will eliminate certain kinds of work. Not all

work, but specific categories of it. Work that

is repetitive, pattern-based, and can be described

in rules. Data entry. Basic content generation.

Certain kinds of coding. Some customer service.

Some legal research. Some accounting.

If you are studying one of these fields purely for

the job at the end, you need to think carefully about

what the job looks like in five years. Not to panic,

but to plan.

The people most at risk are not the least educated.

They are the people in the middle, trained for

specific tasks, in fields that are being automated

faster than the training institutions have noticed.

What It Actually Opens Up

Here is what the conversation usually misses.

AI is the first major technological shift in history

that arrived in developing countries at roughly the

same time as everywhere else. Not ten years later.

Not filtered through three layers of infrastructure

that were never built. Now. Today. Simultaneously.

That matters more than people are saying.

A student in Lagos with a laptop and internet access

has access to the same AI tools as a student at MIT.

That has never been true of any previous technology

wave. Not computers. Not the internet in its early

years. Not smartphones at the beginning.

The gap is still there, in infrastructure, in

access to capital, in the weight of systems that

were built against us. But in the tools themselves,

for the first time, the gap has narrowed to almost

nothing.

That is not a small thing. That is an enormous thing

that we are not celebrating loudly enough.

The Real Question

The question is not whether AI will change your

field. It will. The question is whether you will

be the person who uses it or the person it is

used on.

This sounds harsh. It is not meant to be. It is

meant to be precise.

Because there is a version of this moment where

young people from Africa, from the working-class

backgrounds, from under-resourced schools, watch

the AI wave from the shore. Where they consume

the tools but do not build with them. Where they

are impressed but not empowered.

And there is another version where this generation, our generation, becomes the one that closes the

gap that every previous generation was told was

permanent.

What I Am Actually Doing About It

I am a software engineering student who also writes

poetry. I study cybersecurity. I am building a

website from scratch in a place where the electricity

is not guaranteed, and the internet is not cheap.

I am not telling you this to perform a struggle. I

am telling you this because it is the actual context

in which I am learning to use these tools. And from

that context, here is what I have noticed:

AI does not replace thinking. It replaces the parts

of work that we were doing instead of thinking. The

research that took three hours now takes twenty

minutes. The first draft that used to be a barrier

is now a starting point. The code that needed

debugging for an afternoon can be diagnosed in

minutes.

What is left, what AI cannot touch, is judgment.

Perspective. The specific combination of where you

are from, what you have seen, what you have survived,

and what you care about. That combination is not

in any training dataset. It is yours.

What This Means If You Are Young and From Here

Write. Build. Learn the tools, not to be impressed

by them but to use them. Use them to close the gap

between the idea in your head and the thing that

exists in the world.

The people who will benefit most from AI are not

the ones who understand it best technically. They

are the ones who know most clearly what they want

to make and use the tools relentlessly in service

of that vision.

You do not need to be in Silicon Valley.

You do not need the right connections.

You do not need to wait for the infrastructure that

was never coming anyway.

You need a problem you care about.

You need the tools, which you have.

You need the discipline to show up every day

and build something with them.

That combination has always been enough.

For the first time in a long time, the tools

are actually in your hands.

What you do with them is the only question

that remains.
